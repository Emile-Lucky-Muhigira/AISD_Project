\begin{abstract}
This study develops ElimuSentry, a school-dropout risk model using three waves of the Ghana Socioeconomic Panel Survey. A reproducible scikit-learn pipeline implements source checks, missing-value handling, encoding and scaling, with eight visual diagnostics and exact reconstruction checks for three structurally redundant fields. The comprehensive benchmark representation contains 53 inputs for tree models or 48 for logistic regression. Model exploration compares six benchmark families and two compact logistic specifications. The chosen specification uses 14 substantive predictors, excludes raw region identifiers and explicit missingness flags, and applies median imputation, standardization, regularized logistic regression and development-only sigmoid calibration. It is fitted on 1,049 children, with 258 children in reserved districts. In the primary household-disjoint temporal cohort of 731 children and 76 dropouts, the selected model identifies 30 dropouts among 146 alerts: precision 20.55\%, recall 39.47\%, F1 27.03\%, average precision 0.214 and Brier score 0.0933. The secondary cohort provides a complementary person-disjoint comparison. Final specification choice uses retrospective temporal comparisons; these data are therefore model-selection evidence rather than an untouched confirmatory test. Attrition, limited event counts and survey changes constrain generalization. The implementation supports reproducible research and subsequent independent validation.
\end{abstract}

\section{\textbf{Introduction}}
% ===============================================================
\textbf{Aim of this phase.} Phase 1 used the Ghana Socioeconomic Panel Survey \cite{gsps} and decided how to fill gaps, treat extreme values, remove repeating features, encode categories, rescale numbers and split the data with fold-fitted preprocessing \cite{phase1}. Phase 2 has three tasks, which follow the assignment: (1) implement that strategy as fully runnable code with a strict X\_train / X\_test separation; (2) check every assumption with eight diagnostic figures; and (3) record where reality agreed with the plan and where it forced a change.

\textbf{What is in the repository.} \path{src/transformers.py} holds the custom preparation steps; \path{src/pipeline_builder.py} assembles them into the pipeline; \path{phase2_implementation/notebooks/pipeline_execution.ipynb} runs everything from start to finish, saves the eight figures to \path{figures/} at 300 DPI, writes the processed tables to \path{data/processed/} and the decision log to \path{phase2_implementation/decision_log.csv}. The execution verification at the end of this report records the status of the integrated notebook rerun.

\textbf{Scope.} The analysis covers preprocessing diagnostics, model comparison, probability calibration, intervention capacity, geographic allocation and interpretability. The comprehensive feature pipeline provides six benchmark models; a compact 14-input logistic pipeline is the selected specification. Its parameters and calibrator use development children only. Final specification choice considers retrospective temporal results, so the primary cohort is part of model selection and cannot establish independent test performance.

\textbf{Reading this report.} The opening sections document the comprehensive preprocessing design, eight diagnostics and output checks. The model-evaluation sections describe the selected compact specification, comparative experiments, uncertainty and implementation. The Appendix explains terminology.

\textbf{One pipeline, two versions.} The pipeline is built with scikit-learn \cite{sklearn}. The same steps produce two versions of the data (Table~\ref{tab:steps}). The \textbf{XGBoost version} \cite{xgboost} keeps numbers in their own units, because a decision tree only compares values; it has 53 columns. The \textbf{logistic regression and PCA version} adds the log on long-tailed features and standardises the 16 numeric/ordinal columns, retains 32 indicator columns on their 0/1 scales, and leaves out one option per label as the reference; it has 48 columns. This follows Phase 1, C1 exactly.

\textbf{How leakage is prevented.} Every step that learns something (averages, middle values, neighbours, cap limits, money ranks, the list of options, the scaler) is inside the pipeline. The pipeline is \emph{fitted} on X\_train only and then only \emph{applied} to X\_test. Inside cross-validation it is fitted again on the building part of each fold. The notebook checks this directly: it saves the fitted pipeline, applies it to X\_test, and confirms that nothing it learned has changed \cite{kaufman2012}. The only step applied outside the pipeline, the inflation adjustment, uses a fixed published number, not the data.

\textbf{The split.} Inputs contain 1,307 training and 1,395 later-period children. Primary temporal evaluation is restricted to the 731 children in 605 new households (76 dropouts), with no shared household or person. Secondary evaluation uses 1,175 new people but allows 370 shared households. Five training folds keep whole districts together. Aggregate later-period covariates and outcomes were already explored, so this is retrospective temporal evaluation, not an untouched external confirmation. These subsets are fixed by IDs, not their outcomes.

\textbf{The inflation factor.} Phase 1 promised to convert 2013/14 money to 2009/10 prices using Ghana's consumer price index. The interview dates in the raw survey files show that the first visit took place from November 2009 to April 2010 and the second, labelled 2013/14, mostly from March to October 2014, with a few households in 2015. Using World Bank annual consumer price inflation for Ghana \cite{wbcpi} (index 2009 = 100: 2010 = 110.7, 2014 = 172.6, 2015 = 202.3), weighted by the number of interviews in each year, the price level was 107.5 at the first visit and 174.0 at the second. Every 2013/14 amount is therefore divided by $174.0/107.5 = 1.62$.

\section{\textbf{Design Rationale and Diagnostic Findings}}
% ===============================================================
\subsection{\textbf{Price adjustment and rank interpretation}}
Retain the fixed external CPI conversion (factor 1.62) and learn the rank reference on training rows only. Training-reference ranks bound long-tailed monetary inputs without fitting the later distribution. Previously explored later-period ranks differ from the training median, but that alone cannot identify a causal price or wealth change: composition, measurement and attrition can contribute. No claim is made that inflation explains most of the change or that school policy caused the spending difference. Within-wave test ranking is not used to fit the primary procedure or to choose it from test scores.

\subsection{\textbf{Which repeating feature to drop is not stable across folds}}

The removal list is fixed from feature construction and domain meaning, not from full-training dropout associations. Retain household size and rooms instead of derived crowding; retain parent-alive source fields instead of derived orphan status; retain age and the domain-relevant over-age signal instead of grade. The separate MI-only diagnostic agrees in 1 of 5 folds (Table~\ref{tab:folds}), so it is not represented as a stable selection algorithm. This diagnostic does not change the fixed list. Source identities are checked on all training records, including matching missing values; this is a check of structural removal, not a claim that all preprocessing is lossless.

\subsection{\textbf{Hours missed: an assumption with a sensitivity comparison}}
The retained rule assumes zero missed hours when attendance is recorded; it is not recovery of a known survey answer. It sets 756 blanks to zero and leaves 261 holiday/unknown-attendance cases for the later median fill. The untuned five-fold comparison gives logistic-regression PR-AUC 0.266 for the rule versus 0.275 for median filling, and XGBoost 0.252 versus 0.257. These are descriptive scores with substantial fold variation; no formal equivalence or no-harm conclusion is supported. The sensitivity remains separate; the later source-audited cleaning comparison revisits this assumption under grouped training validation. Temporal test scores must not select it.

\subsection{\textbf{Missing indicators can change meaning across periods}}
Flags are on less often in the later cohort: teacher absent 56.8\% vs 31.8\%, hours attended 21.8\% vs 11.7\%, father's education 7.7\% vs 5.4\%, mother's education 3.8\% vs 1.9\%. Survey mode, coding and cohort composition may all matter; tablet use alone is not established as the cause. Retain the four design/family-context flags as a fixed modelling choice, not because an unadjusted significance test selected them. Compare their behaviour descriptively across periods and retain the uncertainty in transport claims.

\subsection{\textbf{Rank correlation was needed, not the ordinary one}}
The assignment's screening rule is written as $|r|\ge0.85$. To capture monotone curved relationships and reduce sensitivity to extreme values, Phase 1 used the rank (Spearman) correlation $\rho$ with the same threshold. Phase 2 shows why this mattered: for rooms and people per room, $\rho=-0.855$ but the ordinary (Pearson) correlation is only $r=-0.563$, because the relationship is curved (people per room = household size / rooms). With Pearson $r$, this repeating pair would have been missed, and the VIF check also misses it (VIF 1.77). For father alive and lost a parent, both measures give $-0.850$.

\textbf{Final size.} Table~\ref{tab:dims} confirms that the pipeline produces exactly the size planned in Phase 1: 33 features become 30, then 53 columns for XGBoost and 48 for logistic regression, for every child of X\_train and X\_test, with no missing value and no child deleted.

\textbf{Feature count reduced without information loss.} The assignment asks for proof that the reduction from 33 to 30 loses no information. \textbf{No leakage.} (1) Every learned setting is fitted on X\_train only; the notebook confirms that applying the pipeline to X\_test leaves its fitted settings unchanged. (3) Later outcomes do not fit parameters. Final compact-model selection uses retrospective temporal comparisons; primary households and people are disjoint.

\section{\textbf{Selected Compact Logistic Model}}\label{sec:compact}
\subsection{\textbf{Specification and selection}}
The project selects regularized logistic regression with 14 substantive predictors and no raw region identifier. It has the strongest primary-cohort AP, precision, recall and F1 among the eight specifications in Table~\ref{tab:comparison}, while using a compact representation. A 17-input specification adds regional socioeconomic context for comparison. Final choice is based on retrospective temporal evidence; it is not a claim of untouched test performance or universal superiority.

The 14 inputs are age, sex, urban/rural residence, age relative to grade, attended hours, missed hours, teacher absence, travel time, school spending, asset ownership, paternal education, maternal education, household size and electricity access. They come from the starting survey interview. The pipeline fits median imputation and standardization on development children, followed by $L_2$-regularized logistic regression ($C=0.1$), without class weighting. Explicit missingness flags, assessment-status categories and region codes are excluded. Urban/rural residence remains; the specification is region-free rather than geography-free. Unlike the comprehensive benchmark representation, the compact pipeline uses median-filled source-audited numeric values without grade KNN, blank-to-zero attendance rules, money ranks or IQR caps.

A fixed label-independent split reserves 28 of 136 training districts (seed 20261113). Development contains 1,049 children and 146 dropouts across 108 districts; reserved validation contains 258 children and 42 dropouts. Five stratified district-grouped development folds produce out-of-fold probabilities for sigmoid calibration. Each fold fits its own imputation and scaling. All development children fit the final coefficients; development OOF scores fit the calibrator. Reserved and temporal outcomes do not fit parameters.

The context comparison adds development-fitted regional medians of school spending, asset ownership and electricity access, with global-median fallback for unseen regions. These are unweighted eligible-child sample summaries, not official regional statistics. Mean development-fold AP is 0.177 for no region and 0.195 for context. The context variant leads on that internal criterion, while the no-region variant leads on the household-disjoint primary temporal comparison. The final project choice explicitly uses the latter evidence. The same cohort therefore cannot also provide independent confirmation of that choice.

\subsection{\textbf{Predictive results and uncertainty}}
The primary temporal cohort contains 731 children from households absent from the entire 1,307-child training transition, including 76 observed dropouts. The selected model ranks calibrated probabilities and flags 146 children at a 20\% budget. It catches 30 dropouts, produces 116 false alarms, misses 46 dropouts and correctly leaves 539 non-dropouts unflagged. Precision is 20.55\%, recall 39.47\%, F1 27.03\%, AP 0.214 and Brier score 0.0933. Random allocation at the same budget expects approximately 15.2 observed dropouts; the selected model's point estimate is about 1.98 times that expectation.

With 1,000 district-bootstrap replicates and fixed fitted predictions and allocation, the primary 95\% intervals are 0.147--0.320 for AP, 12.8--29.3\% for precision and 25.0--54.1\% for recall. These intervals do not include uncertainty from the final model-selection process. The descriptive calibration plot in Figure~\ref{fig:compact} shows predicted and observed risks. All primary predictions occupy the 0.1--0.2 probability bin under the reported equal-width binning; this coarse summary does not establish calibration across risk levels.

\subsection{\textbf{Geographic allocation and survey missingness}}
The selected compact model allocates 57 of 146 primary alerts to Regions 8 and 10, catching nine dropouts there and 21 of the 59 dropouts elsewhere. \subsection{\textbf{Benchmark explanations}}
TreeSHAP diagnostics explain the Extra Trees benchmark and provide a comparison with the selected logistic coefficients. In the primary cohort, region has mean absolute contribution 0.0759, teacher-absence missingness 0.0320 and maternal education 0.0180. Contributions reconstruct uncalibrated positive-class probability, not calibrated risk; the reconstruction error is below $4\times10^{-15}$. One-hot contributions are summed within each source feature before taking absolute values. This preserves local additivity without defining a separate grouped-feature Shapley game. Deterministic local examples cover TP, FP, FN and TN cases in the saved diagnostic outputs. Explanations describe associations, not effects of intervention.

\subsection{\textbf{Robustness and contextual alternatives}}
A bounded search compares six families, four missingness/geography configurations and three class-weight strengths: 72 candidates. Capacity-focused and AP-focused selection operate within grouped training folds, and calibration repeats selection within its own training subsets. At the primary 146-alert budget, capacity selection catches 20 dropouts, the AP control 21, an equal-weight ensemble 22, and follow-up-weighted Extra Trees 21. Region-held-out inner selection catches 23. These experiments illustrate the consequences of alternative selection and weighting procedures; they do not provide independent replication.

Other experiments examine training-retention rules, anomaly removal, homework enrichment and birth-year discrepancies. Their detailed results, including unsuccessful configurations, remain in the experiment inventory. A work-town grouping comparison covers 11 districts and 186 training children. Matched LightGBM specifications catch 21 primary dropouts with the town grouping and 22 without it. Historical work travel does not establish children's migration routes or commuting areas. These results do not support adding this proxy to the chosen compact specification.

Source audits identify no duplicate outcome matches or contradictory current-enrolment/past-year attendance answers among labelled cases. All 188 training and 145 later positives have recorded grades below the basic-education completion threshold. Birth-year discrepancies occur for 50 training children, including eight positives; exclusion does not consistently improve capture. The compact models use source-audited corrected tables with the same identities and outcomes: vacation code 99 is recognized, a conflicting duplicate English score is marked unknown, and incomplete asset totals are treated as missing. Uncertain attendance alternatives are analyzed separately.

\section{\textbf{Attrition, Sample Size and Validation Limits}}
\subsection{\textbf{Follow-up uncertainty}}
The baseline cohort contains 2,532 children: 1,374 verified outcomes, including 67 basic-education completers, and 1,158 unresolved outcomes (45.7\%). There are 188 observed dropouts. Completers count as observed non-events in the attrition audit; the predictive classification sample excludes completers. Unknown outcomes are never assigned a non-dropout label.

An observation model fitted with district-grouped cross-fitting has AUC 0.614 and Brier score 0.239. Stabilized observation weights range from 0.624 to 2.942, with effective sample size 1,265 among observed children. Follow-up weighting requires adequate observed predictors, positivity and missing-at-random assumptions. Its AUC does not measure or eliminate unobserved selection bias.

If all unresolved outcomes were non-dropouts, baseline dropout prevalence would be 7.4\%; if all were dropouts, it would be 53.2\%. These are worst-case identification bounds, not estimates or confidence intervals. Scenario calculations accompany the experiment. Reported model performance concerns classified outcomes and does not establish population-wide performance or intervention benefit.

\subsection{\textbf{Chronology and effective sample size}}
Wave 1 predictors and Wave 2 outcomes form the training transition; Wave 2 predictors and Wave 3 outcomes form temporal evaluation. All three available waves are already used. Adding labelled Wave 2-to-3 records to training uses Wave 3 outcomes and cannot also produce an independent test of a model fitted before Wave 3. A random partition of these transitions would answer a different question. An expanding-window design with independent confirmation needs a later or external outcome cohort.

The selected model has 14 slopes, giving 146/14=10.4 development events per input column, excluding the intercept. The 17-input context model has 8.6. These are descriptive complexity checks, not universal stability guarantees; a simple EPV ratio does not characterize tree-ensemble complexity. The primary temporal cohort has 76 events. It does not meet a target of 120--150 events, and the development sample does not contain 300 events. Changes to partition percentages cannot create observations or justify weakening chronology.

\subsection{\textbf{Scope of the selected model}}
The final compact specification was chosen after inspecting retrospective temporal comparisons. Its reported primary scores consequently include model-selection optimism. Reserved districts were excluded from coefficient fitting and calibration, but the underlying data had been explored, so they are not a pristine confirmatory holdout. District-bootstrap intervals condition on fitted predictions and allocation; they do not cover all tuning, specification-selection or future-shift uncertainty. Independent prospective or external evaluation remains necessary.

At 20\% capacity, the selected model misses 46 of 76 primary dropouts and produces 116 false alarms. This burden must be considered with intervention costs and available staff. The model is a research prototype supporting further validation and human assessment; no operational effectiveness claim follows from these retrospective scores.

The fitted preprocessing, logistic estimator and sigmoid calibrator are saved together in \path{data/processed/selected_model.joblib}; \path{phase2_implementation/selected_model.json} records the specification, input fingerprints and selection scope. The saved fit uses the 1,049 development children and is the same fit used for reported metrics. It is not refitted on reserved or temporal outcomes. Reloaded-artifact predictions and alert sets reproduce the evaluation outputs.

The scoring entry point \path{src/selected_model.py} accepts harmonized predictor rows, requires unique child identifiers and an explicit money basis, and rejects an outcome column. For 2013/14 nominal school spending it applies the fixed 174/107.5 (approximately 1.62) price adjustment. Other periods require a separately justified harmonization rule. The top-20\% rule ranks a supplied cohort and returns its risk scores and alerts; 0.5 is not used as a classification threshold.

The notebook includes the eight required preprocessing diagnostics, comparative evidence and executable selected-model training. The comprehensive notebook execution record covers 45 code cells; selected-model training and its notebook cell are verified separately. Dated validation records identify the scope of each execution. Tests cover preprocessing, evaluation, group separation, training-only context summaries, scoring schema and artifact reproducibility. The report source, figures, metrics and protocol retain their repository filenames.

\subsection{\textbf{Prospective validation and practical use}}
\textbf{Independent evaluation.} Use a cohort whose outcomes have not informed specification choice. Eligibility should match the research task: enrolled children aged 8--13, with follow-up distinguishing continued enrolment, dropout, basic-education completion and unresolved status. State the prediction horizon explicitly. These survey transitions span several years; a term-by-term school alert system would require a separately defined outcome and validation.

\textbf{Freeze the scoring procedure.} Before examining independent outcomes, record the fitted model, calibration mapping, feature definitions, price basis and 20\% allocation rule. Reuse the artifact's fitted medians and scaling parameters. Any change to preprocessing, coefficients, calibration or the alert budget creates a distinct procedure that requires separate documentation and assessment. This prevents repeated inspection from becoming unrecorded model selection.

\textbf{Check the incoming data.} Confirm consistent survey codes, units, missing-value conventions, spending price basis and age-relative-to-grade definitions. Unique child identifiers support duplicate checks and deterministic ties; they are not predictors. Keep follow-up outcomes outside scoring inputs. Record eligible, excluded and unresolved counts to distinguish performance changes from changes in sample composition.

\textbf{Measure detection and workload together.} At the declared budget, report the number contacted, caught dropouts, false alarms and missed dropouts alongside precision, recall and F1. Also report AP, probability calibration and uncertainty that respects household or district clustering. Random allocation and a simple demographic comparator provide resource-matched reference points. A target of 120--150 observed dropout events is not a formal power calculation. Sample requirements depend on prevalence, clustering and the smallest performance difference worth detecting.

\textbf{Monitor coverage and follow-up.} Report alert shares and errors by region, urban/rural residence and relevant household groups, with denominators and sparse-group warnings. A less concentrated allocation does not by itself demonstrate equitable performance. Track unresolved outcomes separately and examine whether follow-up differs across these groups. Observation weighting and sensitivity bounds can describe uncertainty, but cannot recover the unknown outcomes of individual children or verify missing-at-random assumptions.

\textbf{Use alerts to support assessment.} A flagged child is a priority for further assessment, not a confirmed future dropout. A pilot would need a defined support pathway, staff capacity, record correction and tracking of assistance. Whether support improves retention requires a separate intervention evaluation. These are proposed validation and implementation activities, not completed work.

\section*{\textbf{Appendix: Plain-Language Guide to the Technical Terms}}
The terms below are used in this report; terms already explained in the Phase 1 report keep the same meaning.

{\small
\noindent\textbf{Pipeline:} A chain of preparation steps followed by a model, run in a fixed order. Fitting it learns every setting from the training data at once.\par\vspace{2pt}
\noindent\textbf{ColumnTransformer:} The part of the pipeline that applies the right preparation to each group of columns side by side (for example, a middle-value fill for lopsided numbers and one-hot encoding for labels).\par\vspace{2pt}
\noindent\textbf{Fit / transform:} \emph{Fit} = learn the settings (e.g.\ a middle value or a cap limit) from data; \emph{transform} = apply the stored settings to any data without learning anything new.\par\vspace{2pt}
\noindent\textbf{X\_train / X\_test:} Wave 1 predictor rows with Wave 2 outcomes, and Wave 2 predictor rows with Wave 3 outcomes. Temporal results inform retrospective model selection; independent confirmation requires separate data.\par\vspace{2pt}
\noindent\textbf{Leakage:} When information from the data used for checking sneaks into building the model, making results look better than they really are.\par\vspace{2pt}
\noindent\textbf{Fold, building part, checking part:} Cross-validation splits X\_train into 5 parts; the model is built on 4 (the building part) and checked on the 5th (the checking part), rotating 5 times.\par\vspace{2pt}
\noindent\textbf{Custom transformer:} A preparation step written for this project (in \path{src/transformers.py}) that follows the same fit / transform rules as the standard scikit-learn steps.\par\vspace{2pt}
\noindent\textbf{KNN (nearest neighbours) filling:} Filling a missing grade from similar training children with numerical differences and category matches treated separately; neighbour count selected within training.\par\vspace{2pt}
\noindent\textbf{Capping:} Pulling an extreme value down to a set limit instead of deleting the child.\par\vspace{2pt}
\noindent\textbf{Rank (0--1):} The position of a value among the training values: 0 = lowest, 0.5 = middle, 1 = highest.\par\vspace{2pt}
\newpage
\noindent\textbf{Consumer price index, inflation factor:} The official measure of how prices rise; dividing 2013/14 amounts by 1.62 expresses them in 2009/10 prices.\par\vspace{2pt}
\noindent\textbf{One-hot encoding:} Turning a label with several options (e.g.\ 10 regions) into separate yes/no columns.\par\vspace{2pt}
\noindent\textbf{Standardising:} Putting numbers on a common scale with average 0 and spread 1.\par\vspace{2pt}
\noindent\textbf{Correlation ($r$, $\rho$):} How closely two features move together, from $-1$ to $+1$. The ordinary (Pearson) $r$ only sees straight-line relationships; the rank (Spearman) $\rho$ also sees curved ones.\par\vspace{2pt}
\noindent\textbf{VIF:} How much a feature is already explained by all the others together (above 10 = redundant).\par\vspace{2pt}
\noindent\textbf{Mutual information:} How much knowing a feature reduces uncertainty about dropout; it captures any kind of relationship.\par\vspace{2pt}
\noindent\textbf{PCA, component, scree plot:} PCA combines many columns into a few new ones (components) that keep as much of the variation as possible. A scree plot shows how much variation each component keeps, and the cumulative line how much the first $k$ components keep together.\par\vspace{2pt}
\noindent\textbf{Class weighting:} Counting each dropout several times more during training so the model pays enough attention to the rare group.\par\vspace{2pt}
\noindent\textbf{PR-AUC, ROC-AUC:} Scores from 0 to 1 for how well a model puts real dropouts at the top of its risk list. The AP reference is the evaluated cohort prevalence (0.144 in training, 0.104 in the primary later cohort); the ROC-AUC reference is 0.5.\par\vspace{2pt}
\noindent\textbf{Calibration:} Adjusting risk scores toward observed event frequencies using training-only held-out predictions. Calibration can fail under a later distribution shift.\par\vspace{2pt}
\noindent\textbf{Tie-break rule:} The Phase 1 rule used when two repeating features carry similar information: keep the one measured directly and remove the one calculated from others.\par
}
