# Regional and historical work-town grouping

Official-region pooling already exists in the default predictor set: district identifiers are grouping metadata, not model features. This experiment compares the existing region representation with removing only the downstream region predictor (KNN still uses region), removing all geography, and adding a shared work-town group. It does not invent adjacency between district codes or fit separate small models per district.

Town groups use Wave 1 migration-history answers (s1gii.dta), joined to the household geographic key. A district is assigned the uniquely most common named town across at least five distinct households reporting work-related travel (job transfer, seeking employment, own business, spouse employment or learning a trade). Other urban/rural codes are excluded rather than guessed. Town history is baseline context, not a child's subsequent destination. Full baseline survey context, including held-out districts, defines the outcome-blind mapping; this assumes comparable contextual data are available at prediction time. This is not strict unseen-context validation. Coverage is 11 districts and 186 of 1,307 training children. Unmapped children remain eligible with an unknown group.

Six fixed model families, five identical district-grouped folds and three repeated splits produced 360 fits. All learned preprocessing stays inside each fit; all validation children remain eligible; allocation remains 20 percent within folds. The region-only removal control was added after initial comparison to separate region effects from broader geography removal. No temporal outcomes select groups or models. Repeated splits reuse the same children and do not establish independent confirmation. Results are uncalibrated fixed-setting training comparisons, not replacements for tuned/calibrated temporal tables.

Official region information improves mean capture over the region-only removal control for all six families. Extra Trees catches 77 versus 66 of 188 on average. Adding the work-town proxy gives small mixed gains: Extra Trees 77 to 77.33, Random Forest 75.67 to 76.33, LightGBM 61 to 62.67, XGBoost 62.33 to 62.67, ElasticNet unchanged and CatBoost 65.67 to 65.33. These findings support retaining existing region information in this training comparison; they do not resolve known temporal drift. They do not establish a deployable improvement from town grouping.

A verified rural-to-town catchment grouping cannot be established from these histories alone: current residence is not necessarily the origin of earlier travel, other towns are incompletely standardised, and adjacency/commuting flows are not verified. The proxy tests the available signal, not the full migration hypothesis. All original project data and frozen results remain unchanged.

Run geographic_grouping.py with the project environment. It resumes completed seed/variant blocks; delete or move its aggregate result folder before changing definitions and rerunning. Aggregate support counts contain no child identities.

## Applied LightGBM challenger

Run `python phase2_implementation/experiments/geographic_challenger.py` after generating district_town_support.csv. This compares fixed-setting LightGBM with and without the town feature; both use five district-grouped training OOF predictions for sigmoid calibration. No later labels fit preprocessing, models or calibrators. The same 20% policy applies. This is not a replacement for the original nested-tuned six-model evaluation. Primary results: original 22 TP,124 FP,54 FN versus town-enhanced 21 TP,125 FP,55 FN. Secondary: both 33 TP,202 FP,82 FN. Mapping covers 99/731 primary and 176/1175 secondary children. Historical temporal data are already inspected; no confirmatory or deployment claim. The frozen Extra Trees model remains unchanged.

Source-integrity investigation: neighbouring folder `source_integrity_results` documents completion and duplicate-record checks and 180 fits; no label changes or substantial reliable gain were established.
